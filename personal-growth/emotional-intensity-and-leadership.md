# Emotional Intensity and Leadership

Date: 2026-09-17

## Observation

I tend to approach many areas of life with high emotional and cognitive intensity.

Situations that could remain relatively ordinary or transactional can consume a disproportionate amount of thought, intention and focus. This has appeared both in community/personal situations and at work.

The problem is not intensity itself. Intensity can be a strength: it supports curiosity, commitment, deep analysis, learning and perseverance.

The risk appears when almost everything receives the same high level of attention.

## Development Goal

The goal is **not to change my underlying nature or become indifferent**.

The goal is to develop the ability to **regulate how much mental and emotional bandwidth each situation deserves**.

A useful principle:

> This matters to me, but it does not need to possess me.

Another formulation:

> Let the missiles explode on the surface, not inside. Depersonalize without becoming insensitive.

## Why this matters professionally

If I aspire to operate as a Senior Engineer, lead high-budget projects, or build a product that grows into a significant business, technical ability alone is insufficient.

Greater responsibility brings more simultaneous pressure: production incidents, unhappy customers, mistakes, financial decisions, disagreements, uncertainty and competing priorities.

If every event reaches my deepest emotional layer, it consumes executive capacity that should remain available for analysis, prioritization and decision-making.

Therefore, emotional regulation is part of my professional development.

## Working Model: Intensity Levels

### Level 1 — Transactional
Attend, execute, respond and move on. No extended analysis is required.

### Level 2 — Important
Requires attention and deliberate decisions, but within defined boundaries and time.

### Level 3 — Central
Deserves sustained deep thought. Only a small number of matters should occupy this level simultaneously.

## Practical Experiment

Before allowing an event to consume significant mental space, ask:

**What level does this actually deserve: 1, 2 or 3?**

The objective is not to feel less.

The objective is to become capable of carrying greater responsibility **without living permanently under its weight**.
