# Role Target Analysis

## Strategic question

> What high-impact individual-contributor technology roles can realistically reach or exceed USD 10,000/month, what does the market expect from people in those roles, and what is the objective gap between those expectations and my current professional profile?

This question is the market-facing compass for **My Seniority Project**.

The project should not optimize for collecting courses, certifications, or technologies in isolation. Development effort should be justified by its contribution to closing a demonstrated market gap or strengthening a valuable differentiator.

## Target constraint

The primary search space is **high-impact individual-contributor work** rather than people management.

The initial research should not assume the answer is a specific title. Candidate roles may include, but are not limited to:

- Staff / Principal Software Engineer
- Software / Solutions / Enterprise Architect
- Principal Technical Consultant
- Senior specialist roles in enterprise platforms, banking, cloud, data, integration, or adjacent domains

Roles should remain in the analysis only when compensation evidence and market demand support them.

## Five deliverables

### 1. Target Role Map

Build an evidence-based map of technology IC roles capable of reaching approximately **USD 120,000+ annual compensation** (or equivalent total compensation / contract economics).

For each role capture:

- Typical titles and title variants
- Compensation ranges by relevant market
- Remote / international accessibility
- Typical company types and industries
- Seniority level
- Degree of individual contribution vs. people management
- Initial compatibility hypothesis

### 2. Market Expectation Profiles

For each promising role, derive what employers actually buy at that compensation level:

- Core responsibilities
- Technical depth
- Architecture and system-design expectations
- Business/domain knowledge
- Scope and complexity of problems owned
- Autonomy and decision-making
- Communication and stakeholder influence
- Leadership without formal authority
- Evidence normally expected from candidates

Use current job descriptions, compensation benchmarks, hiring material, and other defensible market evidence rather than relying primarily on title definitions.

### 3. Profile Match & Gap Matrix

Compare those market expectations against demonstrated professional evidence.

Classify each relevant capability as:

- **Strong match** — already supported by convincing evidence
- **Partial match** — capability exists but scope, recency, depth, or evidence is insufficient
- **Gap** — meaningful capability is missing
- **Unknown** — insufficient evidence to judge

The analysis must explicitly identify **differentiators as well as deficiencies**. The goal is not to manufacture gaps; it is to determine the shortest credible path between the current profile and a valuable market position.

### 4. Prioritized Gap-Closing Plan

Convert genuine gaps into a ranked development backlog.

Every proposed activity should answer:

> Which target-role requirement does this close or demonstrate?

Possible actions may include technical work, architecture exercises, portfolio evidence, targeted study, certifications, market-facing writing, interview preparation, or production-quality projects.

Avoid learning activities that cannot be connected to a target-role requirement or strategically useful optionality.

### 5. Feasibility & Time-Horizon Assessment

Periodically estimate how plausible the compensation target is over different horizons, based on accumulated evidence.

Assess at least:

- Market availability of qualifying roles
- Current profile-to-role fit
- Severity and number of critical gaps
- Ability to demonstrate existing strengths
- Geographic / remote-market constraints
- Interview competitiveness
- Expected time to close the highest-impact gaps

The assessment may be qualitative initially. It should become more evidence-based as the project accumulates salary data, job samples, experiments, applications, and interview outcomes.

## Working principle

The central loop becomes:

**Market → Target Roles → Expectations → Evidence → Gaps → Focused Development → Market Test**

This complements the project's existing development loop:

**Learn → Apply → Evaluate → Refine → Demonstrate**

Together they prevent professional development from becoming an end in itself.

## Immediate next research

1. Build the first compensation-backed shortlist of IC roles around the USD 10K/month threshold.
2. Collect representative job descriptions for the strongest candidates.
3. Derive a normalized competency model from those postings.
4. Map existing professional evidence against that model.
5. Identify the smallest set of high-leverage gaps that materially changes market eligibility.
6. Use the resulting matrix to reprioritize the My Seniority Project roadmap.

## Decision rule

A course, certification, technology, or project should not automatically enter the roadmap because it is interesting or generally valuable.

It earns priority when there is credible evidence that it either:

1. closes an important gap for a target role,
2. strengthens an existing market differentiator, or
3. preserves strategically valuable optionality at reasonable cost.

The desired outcome is not to *look more senior*.

It is to become demonstrably qualified for work that the market values at the target level.
