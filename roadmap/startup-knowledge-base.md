# Startup Knowledge Base — Research TODO

**Status:** TODO  
**Created:** 2026-09-06

## Purpose

Build a practical knowledge base about software startups so future product and business decisions are grounded in market evidence rather than only intuition, theory, or product-design exercises.

The objective is not to collect startup stories for inspiration. The objective is to identify patterns that can change real decisions.

## Research scope

Study a representative set of technology/software startups from approximately the last five years, including both:

- startups that achieved major commercial success, including unicorn-scale outcomes where relevant;
- startups that failed, shut down, collapsed, or failed to find a sustainable business model.

## Questions to investigate

For each startup, document where evidence is available:

1. What problem did it solve?
2. Who was the initial customer/user?
3. How painful or valuable was the problem?
4. What was the initial product/MVP?
5. How did it obtain its first customers?
6. What evidence of product-market fit appeared?
7. What was the business and pricing model?
8. Was the company bootstrapped or externally funded?
9. How much capital and time were required before meaningful traction?
10. What distribution or go-to-market mechanism worked?
11. What technical or product advantage mattered, if any?
12. What decisions or external conditions were decisive?
13. For failures: what actually killed the company?
14. Could a small founder-led software company realistically reproduce any part of the model?

## Cross-startup analysis

After collecting enough cases, identify recurring patterns across:

- problem selection;
- B2B vs. B2C;
- vertical vs. horizontal software;
- SaaS and other revenue models;
- founder-market fit;
- product-market fit;
- first-customer acquisition;
- distribution;
- pricing;
- bootstrapping vs. venture capital;
- time to meaningful revenue;
- defensibility;
- common causes of failure.

## Deliverables

- Structured catalog of successful startups.
- Structured catalog of failed startups.
- Individual case studies for especially informative companies.
- Pattern analysis across successes and failures.
- A shortlist of startup/problem archetypes that appear compatible with a small software company starting from Costa Rica and selling locally or internationally.
- Concrete implications for future product experiments.

## Anti-intellectualization rule

Every research note or batch must end by answering:

> **What decision does this information change?**

If the research does not affect product selection, validation, customer acquisition, pricing, execution, or another concrete decision, treat it as background knowledge rather than progress.
